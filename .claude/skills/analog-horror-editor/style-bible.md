# Style Bible

## Visual language
- 16:9, 1920×1080 master, 24 fps. Pilot previews may render at 960×540.
- Background: solid `#000000`. Primary drawings/text: `#FFFFFF`. Danger accent: `#D71920`. Optional information accent: muted yellow `#E6C84F`. No green, gradients, or decorative colors.
- Sparse frames, large negative space, centered or deliberately isolated subjects. Prefer hard cuts and short static interruptions over polished transitions.
- Use readable, high-contrast sans-serif typography. Keep warning text concise and hold it long enough to read.

## Motion and texture
- Camera is locked unless a scene plan explicitly specifies otherwise.
- Character movement is limited, discrete, and slightly stiff. Use holds and deliberate pose changes; avoid spring/bounce easing and smooth cartoon motion.
- VHS/CRT texture is restrained and deterministic: scanlines, occasional horizontal tracking tear, tiny jitter, and light grain. Apply texture above the complete scene so text is affected too. Do not obscure legibility.
- No random uncontrolled glitch bursts. Every major distortion has a cue and duration in the scene plan.

## Continuity
Use the approved reusable character and prop assets. Record design changes in the asset plan and get approval before changing a recurring design. Keep prop scale, line weight, screen-space positions, and spelling consistent across scenes.
